Reconstruction overview

Sensing principle.

A soft gel deforms on contact. RGB illumination lets a camera measure that deformation and recover the local surface geometry.

Rolling mechanism.

Optical core.

A camera, RGB lighting, and battery inside the sensing sphere.

Sensor anatomy.

A bearing-supported gel sphere around a magnetically suspended optical module.

Single-frame
reconstruction.

Physical contact, tactile images, and reconstructed geometry across six surfaces.

Continuous
reconstruction.

GelSLAM tracks overlapping tactile observations and fuses them into a larger 3D surface, refining alignment through loop closure.

Experiments & results.

Comparing specular and matte coatings.

Local reconstruction.

A hexagonal indenter is tested at 50 locations across the sensing surface. Specular coating produces more consistent surface-normal estimates.

Mean normal dot product · closer to 1 is better

Large-surface reconstruction.

A 75 × 32.5 mm feather pattern is reconstructed by rolling. Twenty-four corresponding landmarks measure planar drift.

1.07 mmSpecular
1.25 mmMatte

Distance MAE · angular MAE: 1.3° / 2.7°

Normal force estimation.

A model estimates force from tactile images. Approximately 750 samples from eight indenters are split 80/20 for training and testing against force–torque sensor measurements.
